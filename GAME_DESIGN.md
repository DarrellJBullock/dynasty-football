# Dynasty Football: Next Gen — Game Design

## Vision

A premium, browser-based American football franchise game. You run a fictional
franchise for decades: build the roster, manage the cap, draft and develop players,
call plays, and watch your decisions play out in a full 3D 11-on-11 simulation with
broadcast presentation.

### Pillars

1. **Your league, your rules.** Fictional by default; fully customizable through roster
   uploads (CSV, Excel, JSON), custom teams, and custom leagues.
2. **Decisions that matter.** Ratings, contracts, depth charts, and play calls feed one
   consistent simulation. The same engine powers Quick Sim and the 3D game.
3. **Readable depth.** Every number (OVR, cap space, development) is explainable in the
   UI. No hidden magic.
4. **Broadcast feel.** The 3D game should look like a TV broadcast: cameras, replays,
   score bug, stats overlays.

## Game modes

| Mode | Description | Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| **Franchise** | Multi-season career managing one team: roster, cap, draft, trades, FA. | M0 (foundation) → M3 |
| **Quick Sim** | Simulate a game/week/season instantly with box scores and stats. | M2 |
| **Coach Mode** | Call every offensive and defensive play; AI executes in 3D. | M5 |
| **Watch Mode** | Spectate any game in 3D with broadcast cameras and instant replay. | M5–M6 |

## League structure (default)

- **Dynasty Football League (DFL)** — 32 fictional teams.
- 2 conferences (Atlantic, Pacific) × 4 divisions (North, East, South, West) × 4 teams.
- 17-week regular season, every team plays 17 different opponents (generated by a
  balanced round robin; 8–9 home games each).
- Playoffs: 7 teams per conference (fewer in small custom leagues), top seed bye.
- Salary cap: **$255M** per team. Roster limit: **53**.

## Custom leagues & franchises

Create a league from **My Leagues → Create league**:

- **Structure:** 1–4 conferences × 1–4 divisions × 1–8 teams (2+ in the builder), 4–40
  teams in total. The total must be even so every team plays every week.
- **Settings:** name, abbreviation, salary cap ($50M–$1B), regular-season length (1–20
  weeks). Small leagues repeat the round-robin rotation to fill the season, keeping
  each team within one home game of an even split.
- **Teams** start from a pool of 40 fictional identities; every city, nickname,
  abbreviation (unique per league), color, stadium, and conference/division name is
  editable. Teams in any league can be edited later from the League page.
- **Rosters:** generated fictional players (53 per team, under the cap), or empty for
  building through roster import and free agency. A 64-player free-agent pool is always
  created.
- **Your team:** pick one; the league's franchise is created and becomes active.

Each league holds exactly **one franchise** (career save), so two careers never share a
league world. **My Leagues** lists every franchise; switching makes it active everywhere.
A league can be deleted — along with all its players — unless it holds the active
franchise.

## Players

### Positions

QB, RB, FB, WR, TE, OT, OG, C, DE, DT, OLB, MLB, CB, FS, SS, K, P —
grouped into QB, RB, WR, TE, OL, DL, LB, DB, ST.

A generated 53-man roster carries: QB 3, RB 4, FB 1, WR 6, TE 3, OT 4, OG 4, C 2,
DE 4, DT 4, OLB 4, MLB 2, CB 6, FS 2, SS 2, K 1, P 1.

### Attributes (0–99)

| Category | Attributes |
|---|---|
| Physical | Speed, Acceleration, Strength, Agility, Stamina, Toughness |
| Mental | Awareness, Play Recognition |
| Passing | Throw Power, Throw Accuracy |
| Ball carrier | Carrying, Ball Carrier Vision, Elusiveness |
| Receiving | Catching, Route Running |
| Blocking | Run Blocking, Pass Blocking |
| Defense | Tackling, Pass Rush, Block Shedding, Man Coverage, Zone Coverage |
| Kicking | Kick Power, Kick Accuracy |

### Overall rating (OVR)

OVR is a **position-weighted average** of the attributes relevant to that position
(e.g. QB = Throw Accuracy ×4, Throw Power ×3, Awareness ×3, Speed ×0.5, Agility ×0.5).
Weights live in `backend/app/domain/ratings.py`. A player whose relevant attributes are
all 73 is a 73 OVR. Changing position re-grades the player with the new weights.

Rating tiers (UI colour): 90+ elite · 80–89 great · 70–79 good · 60–69 average · <60 poor.

### Other player data

- **Potential** (0–99): ceiling for development; always ≥ current OVR.
- **Development trait**: normal / impact / star / elite — controls growth speed (M3).
- **Injury status**: healthy / questionable / out / injured reserve. Out and IR players
  are excluded from the starting lineup (see Injuries below).
- Bio: age, height, weight, college (fictional), years pro, jersey number.

## Depth chart & team ratings

Every position is a depth chart slot. Base formation starters per slot: QB1, RB1, WR3,
TE1, OT2, OG2, C1 / DE2, DT2, OLB2, MLB1, CB2, FS1, SS1 / K, P (FB is a reserve slot).

- An untouched slot is ordered by OVR. Once the user reorders it, the saved order is
  used; players who join that position later (signings, position changes) are added at
  the bottom.
- Players can be **cross-listed** at other slots (e.g. a CB as backup FS).
- The starting lineup takes the top available players of each slot: Out/IR players are
  skipped, and a player starts at most once (slots are filled offense → defense →
  specialists, so the earlier slot wins). The editor explains every skipped player.
- Releasing a player removes them from the chart; changing position removes them from
  the old position's slot.

Team ratings come from that lineup:

- Offense / Defense / Special Teams = mean OVR across **every** starting spot in the
  unit (11 / 11 / 2). A spot with no available player — empty, or only Out/IR players —
  counts as **replacement level (40)**, so short-handed teams rate accordingly instead
  of averaging only the players they have. The dashboard raises a critical alert naming
  the unfilled spots.
- Team OVR = 45% offense + 45% defense + 10% special teams.

## Contracts and cap

- Contract: start year, years, salary per season, signing bonus.
- Market value grows steeply with OVR (`market_salary`, `domain/contracts.py`):
  minimum $750K; QB ×1.6, premium positions (WR/OT/DE/CB) ×1.15, FB ×0.6, K/P ×0.4.
- Payroll = sum of active contract salaries. Signings that exceed the cap are rejected.
- Contracts signed in the offseason start the next season.

### Re-signing

Once the champion is crowned, the **Contracts** page opens re-signing for the
franchise's expiring deals; it closes when the next season starts, and anyone not
re-signed leaves in free agency.

- Each expiring player has an **asking price** — his market value — at the length he
  prefers: 4 years at 26 or younger, 3 at 27–29, 2 at 30–31, 1 after that.
- Offer any salary for 1–5 years. Each year away from his preferred length adds 5% to
  his price. He signs if the offer meets it; otherwise he says no and names his price
  for that length (no penalty for asking again).
- A deal must fit under **next season's** cap: salary already committed to next season
  (contracts running past this one) plus the new deal. The page shows both payrolls.
- During the season the page shows what each expiring player will ask; the dashboard
  reminds you to re-sign once the window opens.

## Roster rules (implemented)

- Max 53 players; creating or signing past 53 is rejected.
- Jersey numbers unique within a team. Signing a free agent whose number is taken
  auto-assigns the lowest conventional free number for that position.
- Releasing a player voids the contract and makes him a free agent.
- Players can be created (custom) and fully edited: bio, position, status, all ratings.

## Playing a season

From the Dashboard or Schedule: **Sim week N** plays the next week; **Sim to end of
season** (with confirmation) plays every remaining regular-season week. Every game —
including yours — is simulated play by play using each team's current depth chart, so
lineup changes take effect next week. Results update standings, your record, and recent
results immediately; every game has a page with the line score, team stats, box score,
scoring summary, and full play-by-play.

## Playoffs

When the last regular-season week is played, seeds are fixed and the Wild Card round is
scheduled. Each conference sends its **division winners** (seeds 1–4, by record) and the
three best remaining teams (5–7). Ties are broken by win percentage, then head-to-head
among the tied teams, division record, conference record, point differential, and finally
a coin flip that is deterministic per season.

- Rounds: Wild Card (1 has a bye; 2–7, 3–6, 4–5) → Divisional → Conference Championship →
  **Championship** at a neutral site (no home-field edge). The bracket **reseeds** each
  round: the best remaining seed hosts the worst.
- Smaller custom leagues use 6, 4, or 2 seeds per conference (6 gives two byes); a
  one-conference league's bracket ends with its own final.
- Playoff games can't end in a tie (overtime continues until someone scores). They have
  full box scores but don't count toward standings or season stats.
- **Sim {round}** plays one round; **Sim rest of playoffs** plays to a champion. The
  Playoffs page shows projected seeds during the season ("if the season ended today"),
  then the live bracket and the champion. Afterwards the season is in the offseason; the
  next season arrives with M3.

**Stats** shows league leaders in 11 categories (passing, rushing, receiving, defense,
kicking; top 25 per category) and a sortable table of every team's offense and defense.
Every player has a page with bio, key ratings, season totals for the stats they actually
record, and a game log linking to each box score. Players who change teams mid-season keep
one combined line, credited to their current team (or last team, if released).

## Franchise loop

```
Preseason → Regular season (17 weeks) → Playoffs → Championship
  → Offseason: re-sign / release → Free agency → Draft → Development & aging → next season
```

Once a champion is crowned, **Start free agency** (with confirmation) runs the
offseason in one deterministic step and opens the next season in free agency:

- **Aging & development:** everyone ages a year. Players under 27 close part of the gap
  to their potential each year (30% normal, 40% impact, 50% star, 60% elite trait, with
  variance); 27–29 hold roughly steady; from 30 players decline, faster each year,
  physical attributes first while awareness and recognition hold up. Kickers and punters
  age four years slower. Injuries heal.
- **Retirement:** from 30, rising with age (2% at 30, 28% at 34, 70% at 37; everyone by
  40). Stars hang on longer, fringe players leave sooner, and unsigned veterans are the
  likeliest to go. Retired players keep their pages and stats.
- **Contracts:** the franchise keeps whoever it re-signed on the Contracts page (see
  Re-signing); its other expiring players leave. AI teams re-sign theirs at market
  value, best first, while the cap allows — keeping room to fill the roster — but let
  fading depth veterans (30+, under 70 OVR) walk. Everyone else becomes a free agent.
- **New season:** a fresh schedule; standings, leaders, and the bracket start over. The
  free-agent pool is topped back up with young players, and the season opens with
  **free agency** (below). The dashboard shows an **offseason recap** (re-signed, new
  arrivals, departures, biggest risers and decliners, notable retirements around the
  league) until week 1.

Over ten simulated offseasons team strength, roster sizes, ages, and payrolls stay stable.

### Free agency

A five-day market between the rollover and the preseason, on the **Free Agency** page.

- **Your offers:** offer any free agent a salary for 1–5 years (it must fit under your
  cap, with an open roster spot). You can change or withdraw it until the day is played.
  The page shows each player's asking price, how many offers he has, and whether yours is
  currently **leading** or **outbid**.
- **AI teams** bid for the positions they lost this offseason (retirements and departures),
  spreading their cap room over the spots still open, at 90–112% of a player's price, and
  chase one clear upgrade a day — a free agent at least 4 OVR better than their best
  player at his position. They never bid on players they just let go and only on 64+ OVR
  players. Unsigned AI offers are refreshed every day, so stars usually go by day 3:
  outbid early to land one.
- **Players decide** when a day is played, best players first: each takes his best offer
  if it reaches his demand for the day — 115% of his price on day 1, then 108%, 100%,
  92%, and 80% on the last day — and otherwise waits. Offers are ranked by money (salary
  against his price for that length, so unwanted lengths cost more) with a small pull
  toward stronger teams (±1% per OVR point of team strength).
- **Play free agency day N** plays one day; **Finish free agency** plays the rest. When
  the market closes, every spot a team still lost goes to an undrafted rookie (age 21–23,
  ~60 OVR with upside, minimum salary) — the placeholder until the draft — and the
  preseason begins. Games can't be simulated during free agency, and free agents can't
  be signed instantly; outside it they sign instantly at their asking price.

## Injuries

Players get hurt in simulated games:

- On every scrimmage snap each of the 22 players on the field has a small chance of
  injury — three times higher for players in contact (ball carriers, tacklers, sacked
  quarterbacks, receivers on catches), lower for players with high **Toughness**.
  League-wide that's about 1.5 injuries per team per game, most of them minor.
- The injured player leaves the game immediately; the next man on the depth chart takes
  his snaps. The play-by-play notes it ("K. Quarles (HFD) is injured: Hamstring strain"),
  and the game page lists every injury.
- Each injury comes from a fictional table with a range of games missed: ankle and
  hamstring strains, concussions, and sprains are usually short (0–3 games); a high ankle
  sprain or broken collarbone takes weeks; a torn ACL or pectoral ends the season.
- Status follows the games still to miss: 0 → **Questionable** (plays next week, healthy
  after it), 1–3 → **Out**, 4+ → **Injured reserve**. Each week (or playoff round) counts
  down one game; lineups are rebuilt every week, so players step back in as they heal.
- The dashboard's **Injury report** lists the franchise's injured players with the injury
  and time remaining; roster, depth chart, and player pages show it too. Setting a
  player to Healthy by hand clears an injury; the offseason heals everyone.
- Injuries draw from their own random stream, so a game plays out exactly as it would
  without them until someone actually gets hurt (still deterministic per seed).

Still planned:

- **Draft:** generated fictional draft classes with scouting fog.
- **Trades:** AI evaluates value from OVR, age, contract, and positional need.

## Custom data: roster import & export

Upload a roster as **CSV, Excel (.xlsx), or JSON** from the Roster screen (Import).

- **Preview first.** Every row is validated and shown with errors (row can't be
  imported) and warnings (imported with an adjustment). Nothing changes until you confirm.
- **Modes:** *Add to roster* fills open spots (53 max); *Replace roster* releases the
  current players to free agency first.
- **All-or-nothing by default;** tick "skip rows with errors" to import only the good rows.
- **Templates** in all three formats are downloadable from the dialog.
- **Export** (Roster → Export) downloads the roster as CSV, Excel, or JSON in exactly this
  format — grouped by position, with remaining contract years — so an export can be
  edited in a spreadsheet and imported back (Replace) without losing anything.

Columns (header names are case-insensitive; common aliases accepted):

| Field | Aliases / notes |
|---|---|
| `first_name`, `last_name` | or a single `name` column (split at the first space) |
| `position` | our 17 positions plus common labels: HB→RB, LT/RT→OT, LG/RG→OG, EDGE→DE, NT→DT, LB/ILB→MLB, S→SS, PK→K |
| `jersey_number` | `#`, `number`, `jersey`. Missing or taken numbers are auto-assigned (with a warning when taken) |
| `age`, `height_in`, `weight_lb` | height accepts inches (`74`) or feet-inches (`6-2`, `6'2"`) |
| `college`, `years_pro`, `potential`, `development_trait`, `injury_status` | optional; unrecognized traits/statuses fall back to defaults with a warning |
| `salary`, `contract_years` | salary defaults to market value; years default to 1 |
| 24 attributes | full names (`throw_power`) or the roster-table abbreviations (`THP`) |
| `overall` | optional; see below |

Ratings: values above 99 are capped (warning); missing attributes default to 50. If an
`overall` is listed, missing **key** attributes for the position are filled so the
calculated OVR matches it — so an OVR-only spreadsheet imports at the intended ratings.
OVR is always recalculated from attributes; a listed overall that can't be reached is
reported as a warning.

Imports may exceed the salary cap (warning), since commissioners often load real-world-
style payrolls; the team simply can't sign free agents until it's back under. JSON may be
a list of players or `{"players": [...]}` and accepts the API's own player shape (nested
`ratings` and `contract`), so exports round-trip.

## Simulation & 3D gameplay

The play-level engine is built (Milestone 2); see
[docs/design/simulation-engine.md](docs/design/simulation-engine.md) for how plays are
resolved, the AI, overtime and playoff rules, and calibration targets. Remaining:

- One deterministic play engine (seeded) resolves each play from the two play calls,
  the 22 players' ratings, and fatigue. It outputs an **event timeline** (snap, routes,
  blocks, throw, catch, tackle) with positions over time.
- Quick Sim uses the engine headless; the 3D client plays the same timeline back with
  animations, so a watched game and a simulated game agree.
- Plays: run, pass, blocking schemes, tackling, field goals, punts, kickoffs.
- AI: situational play calling (down, distance, clock, score) for both sides.
- Presentation: broadcast cameras, all-22 view, instant replay from the timeline.

## Content policy

Fictional teams, players, colleges, and stadiums only. Users may upload their own data;
we ship none that impersonates real leagues or people.
